Property Types in Barnet and Their Structural Considerations

Understanding the structural characteristics of Barnet's diverse housing stock is central to our approach. Each property type requires a tailored engineering solution, and our experience across all of them ensures we deliver the right design every time.

Edwardian Semi-Detached Houses

Edwardian semi-detached properties are found extensively in areas such as Finchley, Hendon, and East Barnet. Built between approximately 1901 and 1914, these homes are characterised by their generous proportions, high ceilings (typically 2.7m to 3m), decorative features, and solid brick construction. The internal layout usually follows a familiar pattern: a front reception room, a rear reception room, a kitchen, and a hallway, with an internal load bearing wall dividing the two reception rooms. In Edwardian semis, this dividing wall often supports first-floor joists spanning from the party wall to the internal wall, and sometimes also acts as a bearing point for a first-floor partition wall above. Removing this wall requires a steel beam designed to carry all these loads safely, with adequate bearings at each end transferring forces to the foundations. The high ceilings in Edwardian properties provide useful headroom for accommodating deeper beam sections, which can be advantageous when designing for longer spans.

1930s Detached Houses

Barnet's 1930s detached properties, particularly prevalent in sought-after areas such as Totteridge, Mill Hill, and parts of Edgware, are among the most substantial homes in the borough. These properties often feature wider plan forms than semi-detached houses, with reception rooms spanning greater distances between supporting walls. The internal load bearing walls in detached properties may carry loads from two storeys above, plus the roof, and the spans can be considerable — sometimes 4 to 6 metres or more. This means the steel beams required are often larger and heavier than those used in terraced or semi-detached properties, sometimes requiring mechanical lifting equipment for installation. Our engineers specialise in designing efficient structural solutions for these wider-span situations, balancing beam depth against headroom requirements and ensuring that the bearing points and foundations can handle the concentrated loads.

Post-War Properties and Later Developments

Parts of Barnet, including areas of Edgware, Burnt Oak, and Colindale, feature significant post-war housing. These properties have different construction characteristics, including wider use of cavity walls, concrete ground floors, and, in some cases, non-traditional construction methods. Our engineers are experienced in assessing these less common structural types and designing appropriate wall removal solutions that account for their specific characteristics.

Wall Removal for Extensions in Barnet

One of the most common reasons Barnet homeowners approach us is to combine a load bearing wall removal with a ground-floor extension. This integrated approach creates dramatically larger living spaces by simultaneously removing the internal barrier between rooms and extending the footprint of the ground floor into the garden.

In a typical Barnet project of this type, the homeowner builds a rear or side-return extension and, at the same time, removes the load bearing wall between the existing kitchen and dining room (or between two reception rooms). The result is a single, expansive open-plan space that flows seamlessly from the front of the original house through to the new extension and out to the garden. The structural engineering for this type of project requires careful coordination. The extension introduces new steelwork — typically a beam spanning the rear opening where the original external wall has been removed — and this must work in concert with the internal wall removal beam. Both beams must transfer their loads safely through the structure to the foundations, and the connection points between old and new construction must be detailed with precision.

ProStruct Design excels at designing these integrated structural schemes. We produce comprehensive structural drawings and calculations that cover all elements of the project, ensuring that every beam, padstone, foundation, and connection is specified correctly. This holistic approach avoids the inefficiencies and potential conflicts that can arise when extension steelwork and internal wall removal are designed independently by different engineers.

For Barnet homeowners who have already built an extension and now wish to further open up the internal layout, we can design wall removal solutions that work with the existing extension structure. This sometimes involves connecting new beams to existing steelwork or reinforcing foundations that are now carrying additional loads.

Combining Wall Removal with a Kitchen Refit

A kitchen refit is one of the most popular home improvement projects in Barnet, and combining it with a load bearing wall removal creates an opportunity to completely reimagine the way the ground floor functions. Rather than simply replacing kitchen units within the existing room footprint, removing the wall between the kitchen and an adjoining room allows you to design a kitchen-dining-living space that works as the true centre of family life.

When planning a combined wall removal and kitchen refit in a Barnet property, the sequencing of work is critically important. The structural work — installing temporary support, removing the wall, fitting the steel beam, and completing initial making good — must be completed before the kitchen installation can begin. This is because the kitchen fitter needs stable, plastered walls and a level floor to work from. Attempting to install a kitchen while structural work is still underway leads to damage, delays, and poor results.

Our project management approach ensures smooth coordination between the structural phase and the kitchen installation phase. We work to an agreed programme that dovetails with your kitchen supplier's installation schedule, ensuring that the structural work is completed and the space is ready for the kitchen fitter to begin without delays. We can also coordinate with electricians and plumbers to ensure that services are rerouted during the structural phase, ready for the new kitchen layout.

The design possibilities that open up when you combine wall removal with a kitchen refit are extensive. A kitchen island or breakfast bar can be positioned where the dividing wall once stood, providing a natural transition between cooking and living zones. Longer worktop runs, larger appliance housings, and improved storage can all be achieved with the additional wall space that becomes available when two rooms are merged. For many Barnet homeowners, this combined project delivers a transformation that fundamentally changes how their family uses the home.

The Importance of Structural Warranties for Barnet Properties

A structural warranty provides an additional layer of protection for homeowners who have undertaken structural alterations to their property. While a building regulations completion certificate from Barnet Council confirms that the work met regulatory standards at the time of inspection, a structural warranty goes further by providing insurance-backed cover against latent defects that may emerge in the years following completion.

What a Structural Warranty Covers

A typical structural warranty for a wall removal project covers the replacement steel beam, its bearing points and connections, the padstones and supporting masonry, and any associated foundation work. If a defect in the structural design or installation causes damage to the property, the warranty provider pays for the remedial work. Cover periods typically range from 10 to 12 years, providing long-term peace of mind.

Why Warranties Matter in Barnet's Property Market

Barnet's property market is characterised by high values and discerning buyers. When you come to sell a Barnet property that has undergone structural work, prospective buyers (and their solicitors and surveyors) will want to see evidence that the work was carried out properly. A building regulations completion certificate is the minimum requirement, but a structural warranty provides additional assurance that can smooth the conveyancing process and maintain buyer confidence. Some mortgage lenders also require evidence of warranty cover for properties where structural work has been carried out within a certain period.

How ProStruct Design Helps

We can arrange structural warranties for all our Barnet projects through our established relationships with leading warranty providers. The cost is modest in relation to the value of the work and the protection provided. We handle the application process, including submitting our structural designs and arranging the warranty provider's independent inspection during construction. On completion, you receive a warranty certificate that sits alongside your building regulations completion certificate as evidence of professional, insured structural work.

Building Regulations Process with Barnet Council

All load bearing wall removal in the London Borough of Barnet must comply with Building Regulations, and Barnet Council's building control team is responsible for enforcement. The process is straightforward when managed by professionals who understand the system, and ProStruct Design handles every step on your behalf.

We prepare comprehensive structural calculations and drawings to accompany your application, whether you choose the Building Notice or Full Plans route. For Barnet properties, particularly the larger detached homes where structural loads and beam specifications are more complex, we strongly recommend the Full Plans Application. This route provides formal approval from Barnet Council before construction begins, eliminating the risk of discovering compliance issues part-way through the build.

During construction, Barnet Council's building control inspector will visit your property at key stages. We coordinate all inspection bookings and ensure that the work is ready for inspection at each stage. Upon satisfactory completion of all work and inspections, Barnet Council issues a Building Regulations Completion Certificate — the official confirmation that your structural alteration meets all regulatory requirements.
