Ealing's Edwardian and Victorian Housing Stock

Understanding the construction characteristics of Ealing's housing stock is fundamental to planning a successful wall removal project. The borough's residential development occurred primarily during two distinct periods, each producing homes with specific structural features.

Victorian Terraces in Ealing

Ealing's Victorian properties, dating from the mid to late 1800s, are predominantly terraced houses built from London stock brick with solid 9-inch and 13.5-inch walls. These homes were typically constructed with a clear structural hierarchy: external walls and one or more internal spine walls carry loads from the upper floors and roof down to strip foundations. The internal dividing walls between reception rooms are frequently load bearing, supporting first-floor joists that span from the party walls to the central wall. Chimney breasts are integrated into many of these load bearing walls, adding complexity to any removal project. Victorian terraces in Ealing are particularly common in areas such as Acton, West Ealing, and parts of Southall.

Edwardian Semi-Detached and Terraced Properties

The Edwardian era (roughly 1901 to 1914) saw Ealing expand rapidly as the District Line railway made the suburb more accessible. The properties built during this period tend to be more generous in scale than their Victorian predecessors, with wider frontages, higher ceilings, and bay windows that flood the rooms with light. Edwardian homes in areas such as Ealing Common, Pitshanger, and North Ealing are characterised by their distinctive red-brick facades, decorative plasterwork, and spacious hallways. Structurally, these properties often feature load bearing walls in similar positions to Victorian homes, but the wider spans and different construction details mean that beam sizing and support design must be calculated specifically for each property.

Later Housing Developments

While Ealing is best known for its period housing, the borough also contains significant numbers of inter-war properties, particularly in areas such as Greenford, Northolt, and parts of Hanwell. These 1920s and 1930s houses have different structural characteristics, including cavity wall construction and different foundation types, requiring a tailored engineering approach for wall removal projects.

Another highly popular project in Ealing's Victorian terraces is the creation of a through-lounge by removing the wall between the front and rear reception rooms. Depending on the room layout, the steel beam may span the full width of the property, typically between 3 and 5 metres, requiring a substantial structural section.

The Structural Calculations Process

Structural calculations are the foundation of every safe and compliant wall removal project. Our process for Ealing properties follows established engineering principles while accounting for the specific characteristics of each home.

During the initial site visit, our engineer measures the wall to be removed, the spans it supports, the type and condition of the existing construction, and any additional loads from above (such as bathrooms with heavy fittings, water tanks, or the roof structure). We also note the positions of any services that pass through or along the wall, including gas pipes, water supply, waste pipes, and electrical cables, as these will need to be rerouted before demolition begins.

Back in the office, our engineers use these measurements to produce detailed structural calculations. These determine the bending moments and shear forces the replacement beam must resist, leading to the specification of an appropriate steel section. For a standard kitchen-diner opening in an Ealing Victorian terrace spanning approximately 3 metres, a 152x89mm or 203x133mm universal beam is typically adequate. Longer spans or heavier loads may require larger sections such as 254x146mm or 305x165mm beams. The calculations also specify the required bearing length at each end of the beam, the padstone or concrete pad dimensions, and any additional foundation support needed.

These calculations are presented in a clear, professional format suitable for submission to Ealing Council's building control department and are accompanied by annotated drawings showing the existing and proposed structural arrangements.

Building Control Notification with Ealing Council

All load bearing wall removal projects in the London Borough of Ealing must be carried out in compliance with the Building Regulations 2010. Ealing Council's building control service is responsible for ensuring that structural work meets these standards, and you must notify them before work begins.

Building Notice Route

A Building Notice is a straightforward notification submitted to Ealing Council at least two working days before work starts. It does not require detailed plans or calculations to be submitted upfront, but you must have these available for the building control inspector to review on site. The inspector will visit at key stages — typically after temporary support is installed, after the beam is in position, and upon completion — to verify compliance. While this is the quicker route, it carries some risk that the inspector may require changes during the build.

Full Plans Application Route

A Full Plans Application involves submitting your structural calculations, drawings, and specifications to Ealing Council for approval before work begins. The council's building control officers review the submissions and either approve them, request amendments, or reject them. Once approved, you have certainty that your proposals comply with the regulations, and the inspector visits are confirmation rather than assessment. We recommend this route for most Ealing wall removal projects, particularly where the structural design is complex or where the homeowner wants peace of mind before committing to construction.

ProStruct Design manages the entire building control process on your behalf, preparing all documentation, submitting applications, and coordinating inspector visits throughout the project.

Party Wall Act Implications for Ealing Properties

The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 is a critical consideration for load bearing wall removal in Ealing's terraced and semi-detached properties. Given that many of the borough's homes share walls with adjoining properties, understanding and complying with Party Wall requirements is essential.

A Party Wall Notice must be served on your neighbour if the load bearing wall you intend to remove is a party wall (shared between two properties), if you need to cut into a party wall to insert a beam bearing, or if you plan to carry out work within 3 or 6 metres of a neighbouring property's foundations (depending on excavation depth). The notice must be served at least two months before the proposed start date, giving your neighbour time to respond. They can either give written consent, in which case the work can proceed without further formality, or dissent, triggering the appointment of party wall surveyors who will prepare a Party Wall Award documenting the agreed scope and conditions of the work.

In practice, many Ealing homeowners find that their neighbours are amenable to structural improvements, particularly if the process is explained clearly and professionally. Our team advises on the correct notices to serve and can recommend experienced party wall surveyors to handle the process if your neighbour does not consent. It is important to note that failing to comply with the Party Wall Act can result in injunctions, delays, and unnecessary costs, so this step should never be overlooked.

For Ealing's Victorian terraces, where properties share party walls on both sides, you may need to serve notices on two neighbours. This is particularly relevant when the internal load bearing wall connects to or bears upon the party wall structure, which is common in terraced housing.

Frequently Asked Questions About Load Bearing Wall Removal in Ealing

What types of wall removal projects are most common in Ealing?

The most common load bearing wall removal projects in Ealing involve creating open-plan kitchen-diners by removing the wall between the original kitchen and dining room, and forming through-lounges by removing the dividing wall between the front and rear reception rooms. Ealing's Victorian and Edwardian terraces typically have these internal load bearing walls, making them ideal candidates for open-plan conversion.

Do I need a Party Wall Agreement for wall removal in Ealing?

If the load bearing wall you wish to remove is a party wall shared with a neighbouring property, or if the work involves cutting into a party wall to insert a beam, you will need to serve a Party Wall Notice under the Party Wall etc. Act 1996. This applies to many terraced and semi-detached properties in Ealing. The notice must be served at least two months before work begins, and your neighbour can either consent or appoint a party wall surveyor.

How are structural calculations produced for Ealing wall removal projects?

Our chartered structural engineers visit your Ealing property to survey the wall, measure spans and loads, and assess the existing structure. Using this data, they produce calculations to British Standards and Eurocodes that specify the steel beam size, bearing requirements, and temporary support methodology. These calculations are submitted to Ealing Council's building control department as part of your building regulations application.

How do I notify Ealing Council building control about wall removal?

You can notify Ealing Council building control by submitting either a Building Notice or a Full Plans Application. A Building Notice is quicker and requires less upfront documentation, but the Full Plans route provides formal approval before work starts. For load bearing wall removal in Ealing, we typically recommend Full Plans as it gives certainty and avoids potential issues during inspections.

Can I create a through-lounge in my Ealing Victorian terrace?

Yes, creating a through-lounge by removing the central dividing wall is one of the most popular structural alterations in Ealing's Victorian terraces. A steel beam (RSJ) is installed to carry the loads previously supported by the wall. This creates a single expansive living room running from the front to the rear of the property, dramatically improving the sense of space and light.

Will removing a load bearing wall in Ealing affect my neighbours?

In terraced or semi-detached properties in Ealing, removing a load bearing wall can potentially affect adjoining properties if the wall connects to or shares structure with the party wall. Our structural engineers carefully assess these connections and design solutions that isolate the works from neighbouring properties. Where Party Wall Act obligations arise, we guide you through the notification and agreement process to maintain good relations with your neighbours.
