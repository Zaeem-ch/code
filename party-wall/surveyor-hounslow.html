Terraced Housing and Party Wall Requirements in Hounslow

Hounslow has one of the largest concentrations of terraced housing in west London, and this housing type generates the greatest demand for party wall surveyor services in the borough. Terraced houses share party walls with neighbours on both sides (for mid-terrace properties) or on one side (for end-of-terrace properties), and almost any building work of substance will require engagement with the Party Wall etc. Act 1996.

The Challenge of Mid-Terrace Properties

Mid-terrace houses present particular challenges for the party wall process because they share walls with two separate adjoining owners. This means that two sets of notices must be served, two Schedules of Condition must be prepared, and potentially two separate awards must be made. The cost of the party wall process for a mid-terrace property is therefore typically higher than for an end-of-terrace or semi-detached house, as each adjoining owner is treated independently under the Act.

In Hounslow's older terraces, the party walls are typically solid brick, often 225mm thick, and they may have been modified over the years by previous owners on either side. Common modifications include the insertion of flues, the removal of chimney breasts, the creation of openings (now filled in), and the addition of fixings and services. All of these existing features and alterations must be carefully recorded in the Schedule of Condition before any new work begins.

Common Terraced House Projects

The most common building projects in Hounslow's terraced housing that trigger the party wall process include rear extensions (both single and double storey), loft conversions with dormer windows, kitchen extensions and side returns, chimney breast removals, and ground floor structural alterations to create open-plan living spaces. Each of these projects affects the party wall in different ways and may require notices under different sections of the Act.

Rear extensions are particularly prevalent in Hounslow, where many terraced houses have compact original footprints that no longer meet the expectations of modern family living. A typical rear extension will involve building a new structure that connects to the existing party wall at the rear corner, and the extension foundations may pass within the Section 6 distance of the neighbouring property's foundations. Both Section 2 and Section 6 notices may therefore be required.

New Developments Near Heathrow

The areas of Hounslow closest to Heathrow Airport have experienced substantial development activity in recent years, with new residential schemes transforming former industrial and commercial sites into modern housing. Areas such as Feltham, Hatton Cross, and Cranford have seen particular growth, with a mix of houses and apartment blocks being constructed to meet London's housing demand.

Party wall considerations for new developments differ in some respects from those affecting existing residential properties. When a developer builds adjacent to existing properties, they must serve party wall notices on all affected adjoining owners before commencing any work that falls within the scope of the Act. The scale of these developments means that multiple adjoining owners may be affected, requiring a systematic and efficient approach to the notice and award process.

Conversely, owners of existing properties near new developments may receive party wall notices from developers and need to understand their rights under the Act. As an adjoining owner, you are entitled to have your property surveyed and its condition recorded before the development work begins, and a Party Wall Award will set out the protections and remedies available to you if damage occurs. ProStruct Design regularly acts on behalf of adjoining owners in these situations, ensuring that their interests are properly protected.

Modern new-build properties within developments may also generate party wall requirements when individual owners wish to carry out alterations. Terraced and semi-detached new-build houses share party walls just like their older counterparts, and the Act applies equally regardless of the age of the property. The main difference is that the construction details of modern party walls are typically well-documented and comply with current Building Regulations for fire and sound insulation.

Hounslow Council and Planning Obligations

The London Borough of Hounslow Council serves as both the local planning authority and the Building Control body for the area. It is essential for homeowners to understand that these functions are entirely separate from the party wall process, which is governed by the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 and administered by appointed surveyors rather than by the council.

Hounslow Council determines planning applications under the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 and its associated regulations. Many residential extensions and alterations in Hounslow fall within permitted development rights, meaning that formal planning permission is not required. However, permitted development status does not exempt you from the party wall process. If your project affects a shared wall, boundary, or involves excavation near a neighbouring building, you must comply with the Party Wall Act regardless of your planning status.

Building Regulations approval, also administered by Hounslow Council or approved inspectors, is a further separate requirement. Building Regulations ensure that construction work meets minimum standards for structural safety, fire protection, and energy efficiency. Again, Building Regulations compliance does not satisfy party wall obligations, and vice versa. All three requirements - planning, Building Regulations, and party wall - must be addressed independently.

How much does a party wall surveyor cost in Hounslow?

Separate surveyors for more complex projects may cost £1,500-£2,500. Mid-terrace properties affecting two adjoining owners will incur higher fees than end-of-terrace or semi-detached properties. Full details are available on our fees page.

Do I need a party wall notice for a rear extension in Hounslow?

In most cases, yes. If your rear extension connects to or bears on a party wall shared with your neighbour, a Section 2 notice is required. If the extension foundations are within 3 metres of your neighbour's building and below the level of their foundations, a Section 6 notice is also needed. Hounslow's extensive terraced housing stock means that the vast majority of rear extensions involve party wall considerations on at least one side, and often both sides for mid-terrace properties.

Are there special party wall considerations for properties near Heathrow?

Properties in areas near Heathrow such as Cranford, Hatton, and parts of Feltham may have construction characteristics influenced by their proximity to the airport, including enhanced sound insulation and specific glazing. These features are carefully noted in the Schedule of Condition. The party wall process itself follows the same statutory framework, but our surveyors take particular care to record and protect these specialist construction elements.

Can Hounslow Council help with party wall disputes?

No, the London Borough of Hounslow does not administer or adjudicate party wall matters. The council handles planning permission and Building Regulations, which are entirely separate from the Party Wall etc. Act 1996. Party wall disputes are resolved through the appointment of qualified surveyors who produce a binding Party Wall Award. If you have received a notice or need to serve one, contact a party wall surveyor directly.

What types of Hounslow properties most commonly need party wall services?

Hounslow's large stock of Victorian and Edwardian terraced housing generates the highest demand for party wall services, as these properties share walls on both sides. Semi-detached inter-war housing in areas like Heston and Cranford is also a significant source of instructions. New-build developments near Heathrow and along the Great West Road corridor create additional demand, both from developers and from existing homeowners affected by nearby construction.
