Frequently Asked Questions

What are building regulation drawings and when do I need them?

Building regulation drawings are detailed architectural plans that demonstrate how your proposed building work complies with the Building Regulations 2010 (as amended). You need them for most structural alterations including extensions, loft conversions, load bearing wall removals, new kitchens with structural changes, and changes of use. These drawings cover structural integrity, fire safety, thermal insulation, ventilation, drainage, and accessibility. At ProStruct Design, we prepare full building regulation drawing packages that are ready for submission to your local authority building control or an approved inspector.

How long does it take to get building regulations approval?

The timeline varies depending on the complexity of the project and your local authority. For a Full Plans application, the council has five weeks to assess your submission (extendable to eight weeks with your agreement). Simpler projects such as porch additions or like-for-like window replacements may be processed more quickly. Our drawings are prepared to a high standard with all required details included, which is why we achieve a 98% first-time approval rate. From our initial survey to submission, most projects are ready within two to three weeks.

Do I need building regulations approval for a loft conversion?

Yes, all loft conversions require building regulations approval regardless of whether they need planning permission. Building regulations ensure the conversion meets standards for structural stability, fire safety (including escape routes and fire doors), sound insulation, staircase design, head height, insulation, and ventilation. Even a simple Velux loft conversion that falls under permitted development still requires a building regulations application. We handle the entire process, producing structural calculations and detailed drawings that satisfy building control requirements for all loft conversion types including dormer, hip to gable, mansard, Velux, and rear dormer conversions.

What is the difference between planning permission and building regulations?

Planning permission and building regulations are two separate approval processes. Planning permission controls the appearance, use, and impact of a development on the surrounding area and is managed by your local planning authority. Building regulations, on the other hand, set minimum standards for the design, construction, and alteration of buildings to ensure safety and energy efficiency. Many projects require both approvals, while some may need only one. For example, a rear extension under permitted development may not need planning permission but will still require building regulations approval. ProStruct Design can advise you on which approvals your project needs and prepare the necessary drawings for both.

Can I remove a load bearing wall without building regulations approval?

No. Removing or altering a load bearing wall is classified as structural work and always requires building regulations approval. A structural engineer must design an appropriate support system, typically a steel beam (RSJ), to safely transfer the loads carried by the wall. Building control will need to see structural calculations, detailed drawings showing the beam specification, padstone details, and temporary support arrangements. Carrying out this work without approval is not only dangerous but may also cause serious problems when you come to sell the property, as buyers and mortgage lenders will require evidence of building control sign-off. ProStruct Design provides complete structural engineering and building regulation drawing packages for load bearing wall removals.
