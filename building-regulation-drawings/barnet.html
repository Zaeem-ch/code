Barnet Council Building Control

Building control services in Barnet are managed through the council's partnership arrangements. The building control team handles applications for the entire borough, applying the same national building regulations standards with the local knowledge that comes from working extensively within the Barnet area.

Application Process

Barnet Council accepts both full plans applications and building notices. We strongly recommend the full plans route for most projects in Barnet because it provides formal pre-approval of the proposed construction details. This is particularly important in areas with conservation area designations, where the planning and building regulations processes may be closely scrutinised, and where any non-compliance identified during construction could have significant financial consequences.

Full plans applications are submitted with our comprehensive drawing sets and are assessed within the statutory 5-week timeframe. Barnet's building control officers review the drawings against all relevant parts of the building regulations and either approve them, approve them with conditions, or request further information. Our experience with Barnet's assessment process means we anticipate the areas of focus and address them proactively in our drawings.

Inspection Regime

Following approval, Barnet Council building control conducts site inspections at key stages of construction. The number and timing of inspections depend on the type of work, but typically include commencement of work, excavation of foundations, damp-proof course level, concrete oversite, drainage, and completion. Our drawings serve as the reference document for these inspections, so their clarity and accuracy directly influence how smoothly the inspection process proceeds.

Conservation Areas in Barnet

The London Borough of Barnet contains a significant number of conservation areas, reflecting the borough's rich architectural heritage and the variety of its built environment. Conservation areas in Barnet include Monken Hadley, Totteridge, Mill Hill, Hampstead Garden Suburb, Church End Finchley, and many others.

While conservation area status primarily affects planning permission rather than building regulations directly, the two processes are closely interlinked. In a conservation area, the design of extensions and alterations is often constrained by planning conditions that specify materials, heights, roof forms, and other design elements. These planning-driven design decisions must then be faithfully reflected in the building regulation drawings, which document the technical construction details of the approved design.

For example, if a planning condition requires that an extension in a Barnet conservation area uses handmade clay bricks to match the existing house, our building regulation drawings will specify these materials and ensure that the wall construction incorporating these bricks achieves the required thermal performance under Part L. Similarly, if planning requires a traditional pitched roof form, our drawings show the structural design and insulation details specific to that roof type.

Hampstead Garden Suburb deserves particular mention. This internationally recognised planned community has one of the most stringent planning regimes in England, managed by the Hampstead Garden Suburb Trust in conjunction with Barnet Council. Building regulation drawings for properties within the suburb must align precisely with the design requirements imposed by the Trust, and our team has experience navigating these requirements successfully.

Typical Building Regulation Projects Across Barnet

Loft Conversions in Mill Hill and Edgware

The generous roof spaces in many of Barnet's older properties make loft conversions an attractive option. In Mill Hill, Edgware, and Burnt Oak, the predominant housing types include 1930s semi-detached houses with hip-to-gable conversion potential and inter-war terraces suited to rear dormer conversions. Our building regulation drawings for loft conversions in Barnet include the structural design for the new floor, the dormer construction details, the staircase layout compliant with Part K dimensional requirements, and the fire safety strategy required by Part B.

Basement Conversions in Barnet's Premium Areas

In areas such as Totteridge, Hadley Wood, and parts of Hampstead Garden Suburb, the high property values and the scale of the existing houses make basement conversions an economically viable proposition. Basement projects in Barnet require particularly thorough building regulation drawings because of the structural complexity involved. Our drawings for basement conversions include the underpinning or piling details, the waterproofing strategy, the structural design of the new basement walls and floor slab, the ventilation provisions, the means of escape in case of fire, and the drainage arrangements.

Internal Alterations in Period Properties

Many Barnet homeowners seek to modernise the internal layout of their period properties by removing walls to create open-plan living spaces, upgrading staircases, or reconfiguring first-floor bedrooms. Where load-bearing walls are affected, building regulation drawings must detail the structural solution, typically involving steel beams or reinforced concrete lintels. Our team works with experienced structural engineers to design efficient, cost-effective structural solutions that our drawings clearly communicate to the builder.

Ground Conditions and Foundation Design in Barnet

The geology of Barnet varies across the borough, which directly affects foundation design. The southern and central parts of the borough, including Hendon, Golders Green, and Finchley, are predominantly on London clay. This shrinkable clay soil requires careful consideration of foundation depth, particularly in the presence of trees whose roots can affect soil moisture content and cause ground movement.

Higher ground in the north of the borough, including parts of High Barnet and Hadley Wood, may feature different soil types including Claygate Beds and chalky substrates. These materials have different load-bearing characteristics and drainage properties compared to London clay, and our drawings specify foundation solutions appropriate to the specific conditions at each site.

We always recommend a site-specific assessment of ground conditions for any project involving new foundations. Our drawings reference the findings of trial hole investigations or desktop studies as appropriate, and specify foundation types and depths that account for the identified ground conditions, the proximity and species of nearby trees, and the loads imposed by the proposed construction.

Frequently Asked Questions About Building Regulation Drawings in Barnet

How does Barnet Council handle building regulation applications?

Barnet Council's building control service accepts both full plans applications and building notices. Applications can be submitted online through the Barnet Council website or by post. Full plans applications are assessed within the statutory 5-week period, which can be extended to 8 weeks with your agreement. The building control team reviews drawings against all relevant parts of the building regulations and provides a decision with any conditions noted. ProStruct Design handles the submission process on behalf of our clients as part of our standard service.

Do conservation areas in Barnet affect building regulation drawings?

Conservation area status primarily affects planning permission rather than building regulations directly. However, in Barnet's many conservation areas, the design of extensions and alterations is often constrained by planning requirements that specify materials, heights, and design details. These planning-driven decisions must be reflected accurately in the building regulation drawings to ensure consistency between the two approvals. Our team is experienced in preparing drawings that satisfy both the planning and building regulation requirements in Barnet's conservation areas.

What are common building regulation projects in Barnet?

The most common projects requiring building regulation drawings in Barnet include rear and side extensions to the borough's many semi-detached and detached houses, loft conversions particularly in areas like Finchley and Hendon where roof spaces are generous, basement conversions in larger properties in premium areas, garage conversions, and internal structural alterations to create open-plan living spaces. The borough's diverse and substantial housing stock generates strong demand for a wide range of building regulation drawings.

How much do building regulation drawings cost in Barnet?

Building regulation drawings for projects in Barnet typically cost between £500 and £3,000. Simple projects such as internal alterations or small single-storey extensions start from around £500, while larger or more complex projects such as double-storey extensions, loft conversions with structural dormers, or basement conversions are priced towards the upper end of the range. We provide clear, fixed-fee quotations before commencing any work, and building control fees payable to the council are separate.

Can I use an approved inspector instead of Barnet Council building control?

Yes, you have the choice of using either Barnet Council's building control service or a private approved inspector for your building regulations application. Approved inspectors provide the same legal function, including the authority to issue completion certificates. Some homeowners in Barnet prefer approved inspectors for potentially faster turnaround times and more flexible inspection scheduling. Our building regulation drawings are prepared to meet the requirements of both routes, and we can advise you on which option may be most suitable for your particular project.

Are there specific foundation requirements for properties in Barnet?

The ground conditions across Barnet vary, with London clay predominating in the south and central areas, and different substrates including Claygate Beds in the higher northern areas. London clay is classified as a high-shrinkage soil, meaning foundation depths must account for seasonal moisture changes and the influence of nearby trees. Properties in High Barnet and Hadley Wood may sit on different ground conditions requiring alternative foundation solutions. Our drawings specify appropriate foundation designs based on the specific conditions at your site, referencing NHBC guidelines and site investigation data where available.
