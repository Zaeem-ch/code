LBHF Building Control

The London Borough of Hammersmith and Fulham operates its own building control service. The team handles applications for the entire borough, bringing particular expertise in the types of projects that are common in this area: Victorian terrace extensions, loft conversions, and internal structural alterations to period properties.

Application Process

LBHF accepts building regulation applications online and by post. We recommend the full plans application route for the majority of projects in the borough. This is especially important for structural alterations to terraced properties, where the building control officer needs to assess the impact of the proposed work on the structural integrity of the terrace as a whole, not just the individual property. Full plans approval provides documented confirmation that the proposed work is compliant before the builder begins on site.

LBHF Building Control Fees

Building control fees in Hammersmith and Fulham are structured as plan charges and inspection charges, payable separately. For typical domestic extension and alteration projects, combined fees range from £400 to £900 depending on the project scope and value. These are separate from our drawing preparation fees and are paid directly to the council or to a private approved inspector if that route is chosen.

Victorian Terrace Conversions: A Hammersmith Speciality

The Victorian terrace is the defining residential property type in Hammersmith and Fulham. Built predominantly in the latter half of the nineteenth century, these houses follow a remarkably consistent pattern: a front reception room and a rear reception room on the ground floor, connected by a hallway with a staircase, and bedrooms on the upper floors. The rear of the house typically features a two-storey rear projection with a single-storey addition or outrigger beyond it. This layout provides the starting point for the transformation projects that constitute much of our work in the borough.

Opening Up the Ground Floor

The most popular conversion involves removing the wall between the front and rear reception rooms, or between the rear reception room and the kitchen, to create an open-plan living-kitchen-dining space. Where these walls are load-bearing, which is almost always the case, building regulation drawings must detail the structural beam that replaces the removed wall section, the padstone or bearing details at each end, the temporary support arrangements needed during construction, and the method of forming the opening.

Our drawings for ground-floor openings in Hammersmith terraces specify steel universal beams or channel sections sized by our structural engineers based on the specific loads and span. We detail the bearing arrangements, which must distribute the beam loads safely into the supporting structure without overstressing the existing brickwork or foundations. For terraced houses, we also consider the implications for the party walls, ensuring that the structural modifications do not compromise the integrity of the shared wall with the neighbouring property.

Rear Extensions on Victorian Terraces

Extending to the rear is another staple project on Hammersmith's Victorian terraces. Single-storey rear extensions can transform a cramped kitchen into a generous kitchen-dining space, often with bi-fold or sliding doors opening onto the garden. Our building regulation drawings for rear extensions include foundation details suited to the London clay soil conditions prevalent in the area, the structural opening between the extension and the existing house, the roof construction with its insulation and waterproofing layers, and the thermal specifications for walls, floor, and glazing that meet current Part L requirements.

Side Return Extensions

The side return, the narrow passage alongside the rear projection of a Victorian terrace, is a valuable space that can be enclosed to widen the ground floor accommodation. Side return extensions are particularly popular in Hammersmith and Fulham because they can be combined with a rear extension and an internal wall removal to create a dramatically enlarged kitchen-dining space. Our building regulation drawings for side return extensions address the structural solution for connecting the infill to the existing building, the roof design (often a glazed roof to bring light deep into the plan), and the detailed junction between new and existing construction.

Loft Conversions in Hammersmith and Fulham

Loft conversions are exceptionally popular throughout the borough, offering a way to add a bedroom and ensuite bathroom without extending the footprint of the building. The typical Victorian terraced house in Hammersmith has a cut-timber roof with sufficient space for a habitable room when combined with a rear dormer to provide the necessary headroom.

Structural Requirements

Converting a loft from storage space to habitable accommodation requires significant structural work. The existing ceiling joists, designed only to support the ceiling below and light storage loads, must be supplemented or replaced with new floor joists capable of carrying the loads of a bedroom and bathroom. Our drawings detail the new floor construction, which typically involves steel beams spanning between the party walls to support the new joists, reducing or eliminating the loads on the existing internal walls below.

Fire Safety for Loft Conversions

Part B fire safety requirements for loft conversions in houses of three or more storeys (which includes a converted loft) require a protected escape route from the new loft rooms to the final exit at ground floor level. This typically involves upgrading the existing staircase enclosure with fire-resistant construction, installing fire doors to all rooms opening onto the escape route, and providing interconnected smoke and heat detection throughout the property. Our drawings detail all of these fire safety provisions with the specifications needed for compliant construction.

Dormer Construction

Rear dormers are the most common approach for Hammersmith loft conversions, extending across the full width of the rear roof slope to maximise the floor area with full standing height. Our drawings include the structural design of the dormer, its cladding and weatherproofing details, the insulation specification for the dormer walls, roof, and cheeks, and the window or glazing arrangement. The dormer design must comply with Part L thermal requirements and Part O overheating provisions, both of which we address in our drawings.

Basement Conversions in the Borough

In the higher-value areas of Hammersmith and Fulham, including parts of Brook Green, Brackenbury Village, and Fulham, basement excavations have become an increasingly common way to gain additional living space. The confined footprints of Victorian terraces and the high land values make below-ground construction an economically attractive proposition despite the higher construction costs involved.

Building regulation drawings for basement conversions in Hammersmith are among the most comprehensive we produce. The structural engineering must account for the existing building above, the party walls shared with neighbours, the ground conditions, and the water table level. Our drawings detail the excavation method, the temporary works needed to support the existing structure during construction, the permanent basement structure including walls and floor slab, the waterproofing system, the ventilation strategy for below-ground habitable rooms, the means of escape provisions, and the pumped drainage arrangement.

In terraced properties, the party wall implications of basement work are significant. Our building regulation drawings clearly document the structural relationship between the proposed basement and the adjoining properties, providing the information that party wall surveyors need to assess the impact and that building control requires to confirm compliance with Part A structural safety requirements.

Conservation Areas in Hammersmith and Fulham

The borough contains numerous conservation areas that protect the character and appearance of its most valued streetscapes. Conservation areas in Hammersmith and Fulham include Brook Green, Brackenbury Village, Crabtree, Ravenscourt and Starch Green, Fulham Park Gardens, Parsons Green, Walham Green, Moore Park, Hurlingham, and several others.

While conservation area designation primarily affects planning permission, it has practical implications for building regulation drawings. Planning conditions in conservation areas often require specific materials, roof forms, and design details that must be accurately reflected in the technical drawings. For example, if planning requires that a loft conversion dormer be clad in lead or zinc to match the character area, our building regulation drawings specify that cladding material and demonstrate that the roof construction achieves the required thermal performance with this material as the outer layer.

Our team is experienced in preparing building regulation drawings that work within conservation area constraints while achieving full regulatory compliance. The key is understanding both the heritage objectives and the technical requirements, and finding construction solutions that satisfy both.

Frequently Asked Questions About Building Regulation Drawings in Hammersmith

What building regulation considerations apply to Victorian terrace conversions in Hammersmith?

Victorian terrace conversions in Hammersmith involve several interconnected building regulation considerations. Structural alterations, including wall removals and steel beam installations, must be documented with precise specifications and referenced structural calculations. Fire safety provisions under Part B are critical, particularly when adding a loft conversion that creates a three-storey dwelling, requiring a protected escape route. Thermal upgrades to the existing building fabric must balance Part L requirements with the practical constraints of solid-walled Victorian construction. Drainage modifications for repositioned kitchens and bathrooms need careful documentation. Party wall implications are significant in terraced construction and must be reflected in the building regulation drawings.

How does LBHF building control handle applications?

The London Borough of Hammersmith and Fulham operates a building control service that accepts full plans applications and building notices. Applications can be submitted online through the council's portal or by post. Full plans applications are assessed within the statutory 5-week period, extendable to 8 weeks with your agreement. LBHF's building control team has extensive experience with the Victorian and Edwardian properties that characterise much of the borough and understands the specific challenges and solutions associated with working on these building types. ProStruct Design manages the submission and liaison process for our Hammersmith and Fulham clients.

The most popular building projects in Fulham requiring building regulation drawings include rear extensions creating open-plan kitchen-dining-living spaces, side return infill extensions on the borough's numerous Victorian terraces, loft conversions with full-width rear dormers, basement excavations in higher-value properties near Parsons Green and Hurlingham, and comprehensive period property renovations that combine multiple elements of work. Fulham's predominantly Victorian housing stock provides exceptional scope for improvement projects, and the area's strong property values make these investments worthwhile.

Can I do a side return extension on my Hammersmith Victorian terrace?

Side return extensions are one of the most popular and effective projects on Victorian terraces in Hammersmith. They infill the narrow passage alongside the rear projection of the house, typically creating a significantly wider kitchen-dining space. Building regulation drawings for side return extensions detail the structural connection to the existing building, the foundation design for the new construction, the roof construction (often incorporating glazing to maintain natural light), thermal performance specifications for all new elements, and drainage provisions. Many side return extensions qualify as permitted development for planning purposes, but building regulations approval with professional drawings is always required regardless of the planning position.
