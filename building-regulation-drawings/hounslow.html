Hounslow Council Building Control

Hounslow Council operates a building control service that covers the entire borough. The team is based at the Hounslow Civic Centre and accepts applications via the council's online portal, email, and post. Understanding how the local building control team operates helps ensure that applications are processed efficiently and without unnecessary delays.

Full Plans Applications

We recommend the full plans application route for most projects in Hounslow. This involves submitting our comprehensive drawing sets along with the prescribed application forms and the plan charge fee. Hounslow Council's building control officers review the drawings against all relevant parts of the building regulations and issue a decision within the statutory 5-week period, or within an extended 8-week period if agreed. A full plans approval provides certainty that the proposed construction complies with the regulations before work begins on site.

Building Control Fees in Hounslow

Hounslow Council's building control fees are structured as a plan charge and an inspection charge. The plan charge is payable at the time of submission, while the inspection charge is payable when the first inspection is requested. For a typical domestic extension in Hounslow, total building control fees range from £400 to £900 depending on the project value and complexity. These are separate from our drawing preparation fees and are payable directly to the council.

Heathrow Airport Proximity: Acoustic and Height Considerations

One of the most distinctive aspects of building regulation drawings in Hounslow is the influence of Heathrow Airport. Large parts of the borough fall within the airport's noise contour zones and safeguarding areas, which impose specific requirements that must be addressed in the building regulation drawings.

Acoustic Performance Requirements

Properties beneath Heathrow's flight paths experience elevated levels of aircraft noise, particularly during landing approaches to the westerly runways. Part E of the building regulations addresses resistance to the passage of sound, and for properties in noise-affected areas, enhanced acoustic specifications may be required for new rooms created by extensions, loft conversions, or other building work.

Our building regulation drawings for noise-affected properties in Hounslow specify glazing with enhanced acoustic performance, typically achieving a minimum sound reduction of 30 to 40 decibels depending on the noise exposure level. Wall and roof constructions are detailed with acoustic insulation layers that reduce noise transmission from outside to inside. We reference the relevant noise contour data and specify materials and construction methods that achieve the required acoustic performance while also meeting thermal and structural requirements.

Height Restrictions and Safeguarding

The Heathrow aerodrome safeguarding zone extends across much of the western part of the borough, including Hounslow, Feltham, Cranford, and Heston. Within this zone, there are height restrictions on new construction that are intended to protect the airport's obstacle limitation surfaces. While most domestic extension projects fall well below these height limits, larger projects such as substantial loft conversions or new-build developments may need to be assessed against the safeguarding criteria. Our drawings clearly state finished building heights and ridge levels to facilitate the safeguarding assessment where applicable.

Typical Projects Across Hounslow Borough

Chiswick: Period Property Extensions and Renovations

Chiswick is renowned for its handsome Victorian and Edwardian terraced and semi-detached houses, many of which are located within conservation areas. Building regulation drawings for Chiswick properties must account for the specific construction methods of the period, including solid brick walls with lime mortar, timber suspended ground floors, and slate or clay tile roofs. Side return extensions, which infill the narrow passage alongside the main rear projection of a Victorian terrace, are especially popular in Chiswick and require careful structural detailing where they connect to the existing building.

Basement excavations have also become increasingly common in Chiswick, where high property values justify the significant investment involved. Our building regulation drawings for basement projects include detailed structural designs for underpinning or contiguous piled walls, waterproofing specifications, ventilation strategies, and means of escape provisions. The proximity of neighbouring properties in Chiswick's tight terrace layouts means that party wall and structural considerations are particularly important.

Isleworth and Osterley: Family Home Improvements

The residential streets of Isleworth and Osterley contain a mix of inter-war semi-detached houses and post-war properties that offer excellent potential for extension and improvement. Rear extensions to create larger kitchen-dining spaces are the most popular project type, followed by loft conversions to add bedrooms and bathroom facilities. Our drawings for these projects address the typical construction found in the area, including cavity brick walls, concrete ground floors, and trussed rafter roofs that may require structural modification for loft conversion purposes.

Feltham and Hanworth: Extensions to Family Homes

Feltham and Hanworth in the western part of the borough have substantial residential areas with a mix of housing styles from different periods. Building regulation drawings for projects in this area frequently need to address the acoustic requirements arising from Heathrow Airport proximity, particularly for loft conversions where the new habitable rooms are closer to the noise source. Our drawings specify the enhanced glazing and insulation needed to achieve acceptable internal noise levels while maintaining thermal performance and ventilation.

Heston and Cranford: Residential Improvements

The residential neighbourhoods of Heston and Cranford are popular with families seeking to extend and improve their homes rather than move. These areas feature a mix of 1930s semi-detached houses and post-war council housing, both of which offer good scope for building work. Two-storey side extensions to provide an additional bedroom and enlarged kitchen are a common project, and our building regulation drawings for these projects include all structural, thermal, and fire safety details needed for approval.

Flood Risk Considerations in Hounslow

Parts of the London Borough of Hounslow are affected by flood risk, particularly areas close to the River Thames in Chiswick and Isleworth, and areas near the smaller watercourses such as the River Crane and the Duke of Northumberland's River. While flood risk is primarily a planning matter, it can influence building regulation drawings in several ways.

Where a property is within a flood risk area, building regulation drawings should detail flood-resilient construction measures where appropriate. This might include specifying water-resistant materials for ground floor construction, positioning electrical installations above anticipated flood levels, and ensuring that drainage systems incorporate non-return valves to prevent backflow during flood events. Our drawings address these considerations when they are relevant to the specific project and site conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions About Building Regulation Drawings in Hounslow

Does Heathrow Airport proximity affect building regulations in Hounslow?

Yes, proximity to Heathrow Airport introduces specific considerations for building regulation drawings in parts of Hounslow. Properties beneath flight paths or within noise contour zones may need enhanced sound insulation in walls, roofs, and windows to comply with Part E acoustic requirements. This typically involves specifying acoustic glazing, additional mass in wall and roof constructions, and careful detailing of junctions to prevent sound leakage. Additionally, building height restrictions in the Heathrow safeguarding zone may limit the scale of extensions and loft conversions in areas closest to the airport, such as Cranford, Heston, and parts of Feltham.

How do I submit building regulations to Hounslow Council?

Building regulation applications can be submitted to Hounslow Council's building control team online through the council's website, by email to the building control department, or by post to the Hounslow Civic Centre. Both full plans applications and building notices are accepted. We recommend the full plans route for most projects as it provides pre-approval certainty. ProStruct Design manages the submission process for our clients, ensuring all documents are correctly formatted and accompanied by the appropriate forms and fees.

What types of building projects are most common in Hounslow?

The most common projects requiring building regulation drawings across Hounslow include single and double storey rear extensions, side return extensions in Chiswick's terraced properties, loft conversions across all areas, garage conversions in areas with off-street parking, and internal structural alterations to create open-plan living spaces. In Chiswick, basement excavations are increasingly popular. In Feltham, Heston, and Cranford, family home extensions are the predominant project type, often with acoustic enhancement requirements due to Heathrow proximity.

Are there noise insulation requirements for properties near Heathrow?

Properties within the Heathrow noise contour zones may require enhanced sound insulation measures that go beyond the standard requirements of Part E. Our building regulation drawings for affected properties specify acoustic glazing rated to achieve adequate sound reduction, wall and roof constructions with additional acoustic mass, and sealed junction details that prevent flanking noise transmission. The specific requirements depend on the property's location relative to the flight paths and the noise contour band in which it falls.

How much do building regulation drawings cost in Hounslow?

Building regulation drawings for Hounslow projects typically range from £500 to £2,500. Straightforward single-storey extensions or internal alterations are at the lower end, while complex projects such as basement conversions, large extensions with acoustic requirements, or multi-element renovation schemes are towards the higher end. We provide transparent, fixed-fee quotations before work begins, and building control fees payable to Hounslow Council are additional.

Do Chiswick conservation areas affect building regulation drawings?

Conservation areas in Chiswick, including the Strand-on-the-Green, Turnham Green, and Bedford Park conservation areas, primarily impose additional planning requirements. However, these influence building regulation drawings because the materials, design proportions, and construction details specified by planning conditions must be accurately reflected in the technical drawings. For example, if planning requires matching brickwork or a specific roof material, our building regulation drawings specify these materials and demonstrate that the resulting construction achieves the required thermal and structural performance.
