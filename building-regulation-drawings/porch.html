When Do You Need Building Regulation Drawings for a Porch?

Adding a porch to your home is one of the most popular improvement projects across London and the surrounding areas. A well-designed porch not only enhances the kerb appeal of your property but also provides practical benefits such as additional storage, weather protection, and improved thermal efficiency. However, many homeowners are unaware that certain porch constructions require building regulation approval, and obtaining the correct technical drawings is an essential first step in the compliance process.

The Building Regulations 2010 (as amended) set out the circumstances under which a porch requires formal approval. Under Schedule 2 of the regulations, a porch is exempt from building regulations only if it meets all of the following criteria simultaneously: the floor area does not exceed 30 square metres when measured externally, the construction is at ground level, the porch is separated from the main dwelling by an external-quality wall, door, or window arrangement, and the porch is not heated by an extension of the home's existing central heating system.

If your proposed porch fails to meet even one of these conditions, you will need to submit a building regulations application. This is where professional building regulation drawings become essential. At ProStruct Design, we prepare comprehensive technical drawings that satisfy building control requirements and facilitate a smooth approval process. Our drawings are prepared by experienced architectural technologists who understand the specific demands of porch construction projects.

There are several common scenarios where building regulations become mandatory for a porch. If you are planning a larger entrance area that exceeds the 30 square metre threshold, the entire construction must comply with all relevant parts of the building regulations. Similarly, if you wish to install a radiator connected to your central heating system within the porch space, this triggers the requirement for building regulations approval regardless of the floor area. Opening up the front wall of your house to create an open-plan connection between the porch and the hallway removes the thermal separation and means the porch is no longer treated as an exempt structure.

What Is Included in Our Porch Building Regulation Drawings?

Our building regulation drawing packages for porches are comprehensive documents designed to provide building control officers with all the information they need to assess your project against the current regulatory requirements. Each drawing set is tailored to the specific characteristics of your property and the design of your proposed porch.

Existing and Proposed Floor Plans

We produce detailed floor plans showing both the current layout of your property and the proposed arrangement with the new porch. These plans are drawn to a standard scale of 1:50 or 1:100 and include accurate measurements, wall thicknesses, door and window positions, and the relationship between the porch and the existing building. The plans clearly indicate the floor area of the porch, which is critical for determining whether the exemption threshold applies.

Elevations

Our elevation drawings show the external appearance of the porch from all relevant viewpoints. These drawings include material specifications, finished floor levels, ridge and eave heights, window and door dimensions, and the junction between the new porch structure and the existing building facade. Elevations are particularly important for demonstrating compliance with Part B (fire safety) requirements regarding the proximity of openings to boundaries.

Cross-Sections and Construction Details

Detailed cross-sections cut through the porch structure to reveal the construction build-up from foundations to roof. These sections show foundation depth and type, floor construction including any insulation and damp-proof membrane, wall construction with cavity details and insulation, roof construction including ventilation requirements, and the junction details where the porch meets the existing building. We include enlarged detail drawings at scales of 1:5 or 1:10 for critical junctions such as the threshold detail, the wall-to-roof junction, and the connection to the existing building fabric.

Foundation Details

Porch foundations must be designed to suit the ground conditions at your property. Our drawings include foundation plans and sections showing the depth, width, and reinforcement requirements. For standard ground conditions, a strip foundation at a minimum depth of 1 metre is typical, but we adjust this based on factors such as proximity to trees, soil type, and the presence of drains or services below the proposed footprint.

Structural Calculations References

Where structural elements are involved, such as lintels over openings, roof beams, or connections to the existing building, our drawings reference the accompanying structural calculations. These calculations are prepared by qualified structural engineers and demonstrate that all load-bearing elements are adequately sized for their intended purpose.

Glazing and Door Specifications

All glazing in a porch must comply with Part K (protection from falling, collision, and impact) and Part N (glazing safety) of the building regulations. Our drawings specify safety glass requirements for all critical locations, minimum U-values for thermal compliance under Part L, and ventilation provisions as required by Approved Document F.

Part A to Part P Compliance for Porch Constructions

Building regulation drawings for a porch must address compliance across all relevant parts of the building regulations. While not every part applies to every porch project, our drawings are prepared with a thorough understanding of which requirements are triggered by different design choices and construction methods.

Part A: Structure

The structural integrity of your porch is fundamental. Our drawings demonstrate that foundations, walls, and the roof structure are designed to safely carry all imposed loads, including dead loads from the construction materials, live loads from use, wind loads, and snow loads. For porches that connect to the existing building through a structural opening, we include details of the new lintel or beam and any temporary support requirements during construction.

Part B: Fire Safety

Fire safety considerations for porches primarily relate to the proximity of the porch to the site boundary and the provision of a means of escape. If the porch is within 1 metre of the boundary, there are restrictions on the amount of unprotected area (openings and combustible surfaces) permitted in the elevation facing the boundary. Our drawings clearly indicate boundary distances and demonstrate compliance with the relevant fire safety requirements.

Part C: Site Preparation and Resistance to Contaminants and Moisture

The porch floor and walls must be protected against moisture penetration. Our drawings specify the damp-proof course and damp-proof membrane arrangements, including how these connect to the existing damp-proofing of the main building. We detail the ground preparation requirements, including the removal of topsoil and vegetation beneath the porch footprint.

Part L: Conservation of Fuel and Power

If your porch is heated or is not thermally separated from the main dwelling, it must comply with the thermal performance requirements of Part L. Our drawings specify insulation values for the floor, walls, and roof, as well as the thermal performance requirements for windows and doors. We calculate U-values for each element and demonstrate that the overall thermal performance meets or exceeds the minimum standards set out in Approved Document L.

Part P: Electrical Safety

Any electrical installation within the porch, including lighting, power sockets, and external security lighting, must comply with Part P. Our drawings indicate the locations of electrical fittings and note that all electrical work must be carried out by a competent person registered with a Part P self-certification scheme, or alternatively inspected and tested by building control.

The Process of Obtaining Building Regulation Approval for a Porch

Understanding the approval process helps you plan your project timeline effectively. At ProStruct Design, we guide our clients through every stage, from initial consultation to the issuing of a completion certificate.

Step 1: Initial Consultation and Site Survey

We begin with a detailed consultation to understand your requirements, followed by a site survey of your property. During the survey, we take accurate measurements of the existing building, note the ground conditions, identify any constraints such as drainage runs or overhead cables, and photograph the property for reference during the drawing preparation stage. This survey typically takes 1 to 2 hours and is carried out by one of our experienced surveyors.

Step 2: Drawing Preparation

Using the survey data and your design brief, our team prepares the building regulation drawings. This stage typically takes 5 to 10 working days, depending on the complexity of the project. We use industry-standard CAD software to produce clear, accurate drawings that meet the expectations of building control officers across all London boroughs.

Step 3: Client Review and Revisions

Before submission, we share the draft drawings with you for review. This is your opportunity to confirm that the design matches your expectations and to request any amendments. We include one round of revisions as standard within our fee, with additional revisions available at a modest additional cost.

Step 4: Building Regulations Submission

We submit the completed drawings to your local authority building control department or to an approved inspector on your behalf. For a full plans application, the building control body has a statutory period of 5 weeks (extendable to 8 weeks with your agreement) to examine the plans and either approve them, approve them with conditions, or reject them with reasons.

Step 5: Building Control Inspections

Once approved, your builder can begin construction. Building control will carry out inspections at key stages, including the foundation excavation, the damp-proof course level, the drainage connections, and the final completion. Our drawings serve as the reference document against which these inspections are conducted, so their accuracy and completeness are paramount.

Frequently Asked Questions About Porch Building Regulation Drawings

Do I need building regulation drawings for a porch?

You need building regulation drawings if your porch exceeds 30 square metres in floor area, is heated by the home's central heating system, or involves structural alterations to the existing building such as removing a load-bearing wall to create an open-plan connection. If your porch meets all the exemption criteria -- under 30 square metres, at ground level, thermally separated from the house, and not connected to the central heating -- then building regulations approval is not required, although Part P (electrical safety) may still apply to any electrical work within the porch.

How much do building regulation drawings for a porch cost?

Building regulation drawings for a porch typically cost between £500 and £1,500 depending on the complexity of the project, structural requirements, and whether the porch connects to the existing building through a structural opening. Our fixed-fee quotations include the site survey, drawing preparation, one round of revisions, and submission to building control. Building control fees payable to the local authority or approved inspector are additional and typically range from £200 to £500.

How long does it take to get building regulation approval for a porch?

The full plans application route typically takes 5 to 8 weeks for building control to assess and approve. The drawing preparation itself usually takes 1 to 2 weeks. Using a building notice route can allow work to start within 48 hours of submission, but the full plans approval route is recommended for porches because it provides certainty that the proposed construction complies with the regulations before work begins on site.

What is included in porch building regulation drawings?

Porch building regulation drawings include detailed floor plans showing existing and proposed layouts, elevations of all external faces, cross-sections revealing the construction build-up from foundations to roof, foundation details, structural calculations references, insulation specifications for Part L compliance, drainage plans, glazing specifications compliant with Part K and Part N, and ventilation details as required by Part F. Each drawing set is tailored to the specific requirements of your project.

Can I build a porch without building regulations?

You can build a porch without building regulations approval only if it has a floor area of 30 square metres or less measured externally, is at ground level, is separated from the rest of the house by an external-quality wall and door, and is not heated by the home's central heating system. Even where the porch itself is exempt, any electrical work within the porch must comply with Part P, and if you are making structural changes to the existing building to accommodate the porch, those changes will require building regulations approval.

Do I need planning permission as well as building regulations for a porch?

Planning permission and building regulations are separate legal requirements. Many porches fall under permitted development rights for planning purposes, provided they do not exceed 3 square metres in floor area, are no more than 3 metres in height, and are at least 2 metres from any boundary facing a highway. However, even if your porch is permitted development, it may still require building regulations approval. In conservation areas or on listed buildings, additional planning constraints apply. We can advise you on both requirements during our initial consultation.
