Harrow Council Building Control

Harrow Council maintains an in-house building control team that serves the entire borough. The team processes building regulation applications, conducts site inspections during construction, and issues completion certificates when work is satisfactorily completed. Our familiarity with Harrow's building control procedures ensures that the drawings we prepare align with the team's expectations and requirements.

The Application Process in Harrow

Harrow Council accepts applications through its online planning portal and by post. For building regulation drawings, the full plans application route is our recommended approach. This provides a formal assessment of the proposed construction against all relevant regulatory requirements before work begins, giving both the homeowner and the builder confidence that the design is compliant.

Upon submission of a full plans application with our drawings, Harrow's building control officers examine the proposals within the statutory timeframe. Their assessment covers structural adequacy, fire safety, thermal performance, moisture resistance, drainage, and all other applicable requirements. Where they have queries or require additional information, we respond on behalf of our clients, drawing on our experience of the specific points that Harrow's team typically focuses on.

Building Control Fees

Harrow Council's building control fees are structured similarly to other London boroughs, with a plan charge payable at submission and an inspection charge payable at the commencement of work on site. For typical domestic projects in Harrow, combined building control fees range from £350 to £800. These fees are separate from our drawing preparation fees and are paid directly to the council.

Conservation Areas in Harrow

Harrow contains a number of designated conservation areas that reflect the borough's historic and architectural significance. Understanding these designations is important because they influence design decisions that are then documented in the building regulation drawings.

Pinner Conservation Areas

Pinner is one of Harrow's most historically significant areas, with the Pinner High Street Conservation Area and the Pinner Hill Estate Conservation Area preserving the character of this attractive village setting. Building projects in Pinner must be designed with sensitivity to the existing streetscape and architectural character. While this is primarily a planning consideration, it directly affects the building regulation drawings because the materials, proportions, and construction methods specified in the planning approval must be accurately detailed in the technical drawings. Our team is experienced in preparing building regulation drawings for Pinner properties that achieve compliance while respecting the area's character.

Harrow on the Hill

The Harrow on the Hill Conservation Area encompasses the historic hilltop settlement around St Mary's Church and Harrow School. Properties in this area range from medieval origins to Georgian, Victorian, and later periods. Building regulation drawings for alterations and extensions to these properties require particular care in specifying construction details that are appropriate to the building's age and construction type. For example, solid-walled historic buildings may require different insulation approaches compared to modern cavity-walled construction, and our drawings address these distinctions.

Stanmore and Canons Park

The Stanmore and Little Stanmore Conservation Areas, together with the Canons Park area, contain significant historic buildings and mature landscapes. Development in these areas is carefully controlled, and our building regulation drawings for Stanmore projects reflect the design requirements that emerge from the conservation area planning process.

Typical Building Projects in Harrow

Rear Extensions Across the Borough

Rear extensions remain the single most common project requiring building regulation drawings in Harrow. The borough's extensive stock of semi-detached and detached houses, particularly in areas like Rayners Lane, North Harrow, and South Harrow, provides ample scope for rear extension projects. Single-storey extensions to create larger kitchen-dining areas are the most popular configuration, but we also prepare drawings for double-storey rear extensions that add bedroom and bathroom space at first floor level. Our drawings for Harrow rear extensions typically include foundation details suited to the local clay ground conditions, structural specifications for the opening between the extension and the existing house, and thermal performance details demonstrating Part L compliance.

Loft Conversions in Harrow

The generous roof spaces found in many of Harrow's inter-war houses make loft conversions an attractive proposition. Hip-to-gable conversions, where the sloping side of the roof is built out to a vertical gable wall, are particularly popular on semi-detached houses as they significantly increase the usable floor area in the new loft rooms. Our building regulation drawings for Harrow loft conversions address the full range of requirements including structural floor and roof alterations, staircase design and positioning, fire safety provisions including protected escape routes, window specifications for means of escape, and thermal insulation of the new roof envelope.

Side Extensions

Many semi-detached and detached properties in Harrow have side access that can accommodate a side extension. These projects require careful consideration of the relationship with the neighbouring property, boundary distances for fire safety purposes, and the visual impact on the streetscene. Our building regulation drawings detail the structural independence of the new construction from any party wall, the fire resistance of the elevation facing the boundary, and the thermal and drainage provisions required for compliance.

Garage Conversions

Converting an integral or attached garage into a habitable room is a cost-effective way to gain additional living space. In Harrow, where many properties have garages that are underused for vehicle storage, this is an increasingly popular project. Building regulation drawings for garage conversions in Harrow show the new front wall construction, floor insulation upgrades, damp-proofing measures, and any structural modifications needed to support the floor above if converting an integral garage.

Ground Conditions and Foundation Design in Harrow

The ground conditions across the London Borough of Harrow are predominantly London clay, a highly shrinkable soil type that demands careful foundation design. The clay's moisture content fluctuates seasonally, causing the ground to heave in wet conditions and shrink in dry conditions. This movement is amplified in the vicinity of trees, whose root systems extract moisture from the soil and can cause significant ground movement affecting building foundations.

Harrow is a well-treed borough with many mature specimens in both public and private land. Our building regulation drawings take account of the species, maturity, and proximity of nearby trees when specifying foundation depths. Following the NHBC guidelines for foundations in shrinkable clay, we specify depths that provide adequate protection against the effects of tree-related ground movement. In some cases, particularly where large trees are close to the proposed construction, we may specify piled foundations or other engineered solutions.

The higher ground around Harrow on the Hill may have different substrate characteristics compared to the lower-lying areas to the south and east. Our drawings always reflect the specific ground conditions at the project site, based on available geological data and, where necessary, site investigation findings.

Frequently Asked Questions About Building Regulation Drawings in Harrow

How does Harrow Council's building control service work?

Harrow Council operates an in-house building control service that accepts full plans applications and building notices. Applications can be submitted online through the council's planning portal or by post. The building control team examines submissions within the statutory 5-week period, extendable to 8 weeks with your agreement. They assess the drawings against all relevant parts of the building regulations and issue a formal decision. ProStruct Design manages the submission and liaison process on behalf of our Harrow clients as standard.

Are there conservation areas in Harrow that affect building projects?

Yes, Harrow has several designated conservation areas including Pinner High Street, Pinner Hill Estate, Old Church Lane, Harrow on the Hill, Stanmore, Little Stanmore, and others. While conservation area designation primarily affects planning permission requirements, the design constraints it imposes have a direct influence on building regulation drawings. Materials, roof forms, window styles, and proportions specified by planning conditions or conservation area design guidance must be accurately reflected in the technical building regulation submissions.

What building projects are popular in Harrow?

The most popular building projects in Harrow requiring building regulation drawings include single and double storey rear extensions, side extensions to semi-detached properties, loft conversions with rear dormers or hip-to-gable alterations, garage conversions, and internal structural alterations to create open-plan living spaces. In areas like Stanmore and Pinner, where properties tend to be larger, comprehensive renovation and extension projects combining multiple elements are also common.

How much do building regulation drawings cost in Harrow?

Our fees for building regulation drawings in Harrow typically range from £500 to £2,500, depending on the type and complexity of the project. Simple projects such as garage conversions or internal structural alterations are at the lower end, while larger extensions, loft conversions with structural alterations, and multi-element renovation schemes are priced towards the upper end. We always provide a clear, fixed-fee quotation before commencing work. Building control fees payable to Harrow Council are additional and typically range from £350 to £800.

What ground conditions should I expect in Harrow?

Harrow sits predominantly on London clay, a highly shrinkable soil type that requires careful foundation design. The clay's tendency to shrink in dry weather and swell in wet conditions, particularly near trees, means that foundation depths must account for seasonal ground movement. Properties near mature trees may require deeper foundations or engineered solutions such as piles. Higher areas like Harrow on the Hill may have different ground conditions. Our building regulation drawings specify foundation solutions tailored to the specific conditions at your property, following NHBC guidelines for shrinkable clay soils.

Can I extend my Harrow property under permitted development?

Many extension projects in Harrow qualify for permitted development, meaning they do not need formal planning permission provided they meet the criteria regarding size, height, position, and materials set out in the General Permitted Development Order. However, permitted development does not remove the need for building regulations approval. Even if your extension does not require planning permission, you will still need building regulation drawings and approval from either Harrow Council building control or an approved inspector. In conservation areas within Harrow, some permitted development rights may be restricted or removed entirely.
