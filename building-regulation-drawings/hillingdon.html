Hillingdon Council Building Control

Hillingdon Council operates a building control service from the Civic Centre in Uxbridge. The team processes building regulation applications for the entire borough, conducting plan assessments and site inspections to ensure compliance with the Building Regulations 2010 and all subsequent amendments.

Application Routes

Hillingdon Council accepts both full plans applications and building notices. For residential extension and conversion projects, we consistently recommend the full plans route. This approach provides formal approval of the construction details before work begins, reducing the risk of costly changes during the build and providing the homeowner with documented confirmation that their project complies with all applicable regulations.

Applications can be submitted through Hillingdon Council's online system or by post. Our drawings are prepared in formats compatible with both submission methods, and we handle the submission process on behalf of our clients, including the preparation of application forms and the payment of plan charges.

Assessment and Inspections

Hillingdon's building control officers assess full plans applications within the statutory 5-week timeframe, though straightforward applications may receive a decision more quickly. During their assessment, they check the drawings against the relevant parts of the building regulations and either approve the plans, approve them with conditions, or request amendments or additional information. Our familiarity with Hillingdon's assessment priorities helps us prepare drawings that address the key points proactively.

Heathrow Airport: Acoustic and Safeguarding Considerations

Heathrow Airport sits within the southern part of the London Borough of Hillingdon, and its influence on building projects extends across a wide area. Properties in Hayes, Harlington, Harmondsworth, Cranford, and parts of Hillingdon village are most directly affected, but the noise contours and safeguarding zone extend further into the borough.

Acoustic Requirements for Building Regulation Drawings

For properties within the Heathrow noise contour zones, building regulation drawings must specify construction details that provide adequate protection against aircraft noise. This is particularly important for habitable rooms created by extensions or loft conversions, where the new rooms must provide a suitable internal acoustic environment for sleeping and living.

Our drawings for Heathrow-affected properties in Hillingdon specify glazing systems with high acoustic ratings, wall constructions with sufficient mass and insulation to attenuate external noise, roof constructions designed for acoustic performance as well as thermal efficiency, and sealing details at junctions and around openings to prevent noise flanking. We reference the noise contour data published by the Civil Aviation Authority and apply the guidance in Approved Document E to determine the appropriate level of acoustic protection for each project.

Aerodrome Safeguarding

The Heathrow aerodrome safeguarding zone imposes height restrictions on new construction and alterations that increase the height of existing buildings. While most domestic extension projects are well below the height limits, loft conversions and new-build projects should be checked against the safeguarding criteria. Our drawings clearly indicate all relevant heights and ridge levels to facilitate the safeguarding consultation process where it applies.

Typical Residential Projects Across Hillingdon

Extensions in Uxbridge and Ickenham

Uxbridge and Ickenham feature extensive areas of inter-war semi-detached housing with generous plots that accommodate extension projects well. Single-storey rear extensions are the most common project, creating larger kitchen-dining-living spaces that suit modern family life. Two-storey side extensions are also popular on detached and wider semi-detached plots, effectively increasing the footprint of the house at both ground and first floor levels. Our building regulation drawings for these projects include foundation designs suited to the local ground conditions, structural details for the connections between new and existing construction, and full thermal and fire safety specifications.

Loft Conversions in Ruislip and Eastcote

The 1930s semi-detached houses that predominate in Ruislip and Eastcote have roof geometries that are well-suited to loft conversion. Hip-to-gable conversions are particularly effective on these houses, converting the hipped side of the roof to a vertical gable wall to maximise the floor area in the new loft rooms. Combined with a rear dormer, this approach can provide two good-sized bedrooms and a bathroom within the converted loft space. Our drawings detail every aspect of the conversion, from the structural steelwork and new floor construction to the staircase design, fire safety provisions, and thermal insulation of the modified roof envelope.

Homeowners who might previously have moved to gain more space are now choosing to extend and improve their existing homes, taking advantage of the enhanced connectivity while avoiding the costs and disruption of moving. Rear extensions, garage conversions, and porch additions are all popular projects in these areas, and our building regulation drawings cater to the full range of improvement works.

Properties in Northwood and Northwood Hills

Frequently Asked Questions About Building Regulation Drawings in Hillingdon

How does Hillingdon Council process building regulation applications?

Hillingdon Council's building control service accepts full plans applications and building notices through the council's online system or by post to the Civic Centre in Uxbridge. Full plans applications are assessed within the statutory 5-week period, extendable to 8 weeks with your agreement. The building control team checks submissions against all relevant parts of the building regulations and issues a formal decision. ProStruct Design manages the entire submission and follow-up process on behalf of our Hillingdon clients, ensuring complete and accurate applications.

Does Heathrow Airport affect building regulations in Hillingdon?

Heathrow Airport is physically located within the London Borough of Hillingdon, and its proximity has significant implications for building projects in the southern half of the borough. Properties in Hayes, Harlington, Harmondsworth, Cranford, and surrounding areas may need enhanced acoustic insulation in building regulation drawings to protect habitable rooms from aircraft noise. Height restrictions within the aerodrome safeguarding zone may also limit certain types of development. Our drawings incorporate all necessary acoustic specifications and height information for Heathrow-affected properties.

The most common residential projects in Hillingdon requiring building regulation drawings include single and double storey rear extensions, loft conversions with dormers or hip-to-gable alterations, garage conversions, porch additions, and internal structural alterations to create open-plan living spaces. In Uxbridge and Ruislip, the inter-war housing stock drives strong demand for extension and conversion projects. In Hayes and West Drayton, improved Elizabeth line connectivity has increased demand for home improvements as homeowners invest in properties with enhanced transport links.
