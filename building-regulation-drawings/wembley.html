Submission Routes

There are two main routes for building regulations submissions in Brent: the full plans application and the building notice. For most projects, we recommend the full plans route because it provides formal approval of the drawings before construction begins, giving you certainty that the proposed work complies with the regulations. The building notice route allows work to start more quickly but without the assurance of pre-approved drawings, which can lead to costly changes if the building control officer identifies non-compliance during site inspections.

A third option is to use a private approved inspector instead of Brent Council's building control service. Approved inspectors offer the same legal authority to assess compliance and issue completion certificates. Some clients prefer this route because approved inspectors may offer more flexible inspection scheduling and faster response times. We can advise you on the most suitable route for your project and prepare drawings that meet the requirements of whichever building control body you choose.

Brent Council Building Control Fees

Brent Council charges fees for building control services, split into a plan charge (payable at submission) and an inspection charge (payable when the first inspection is requested). The total fees depend on the type and value of the work. For a typical domestic extension project in Wembley, you can expect building control fees of approximately £400 to £800. These fees are separate from our drawing preparation fees and are payable directly to the council.

Typical Building Regulation Projects in Wembley

The residential property landscape in Wembley generates a consistent demand for building regulation drawings across a wide range of project types. Understanding the common projects in the area helps homeowners appreciate what is involved and what drawings they are likely to need.

Rear Extensions in Wembley

Rear extensions are the most popular home improvement project in Wembley. The typical semi-detached and terraced houses in areas such as Wembley Central, North Wembley, and the streets surrounding Barham Park often have generous rear gardens that can accommodate single-storey or double-storey extensions. Our building regulation drawings for rear extensions include foundation details suited to the London clay subsoil common in the area, structural designs for the opening between the extension and the existing house, and full Part L thermal compliance specifications.

Loft Conversions

Loft conversions are particularly popular in Wembley's inter-war semi-detached houses, which typically have roof spaces that are well-suited to conversion. The most common approach is a rear dormer loft conversion, which maximises the usable floor area while keeping the front roof slope largely unchanged. Our drawings address the structural alterations needed to create a habitable room in the roof, including new floor joists or steel beams, the dormer construction, staircase design compliant with Part K, and fire safety provisions under Part B including protected escape routes and smoke detection systems.

Garage Conversions

Many Wembley properties have integral or attached garages that owners wish to convert into additional living space. Garage conversions require building regulation drawings showing the new front wall construction (replacing the garage door), floor insulation to achieve Part L compliance, and any structural considerations if load-bearing elements are affected. In Wembley, where on-street parking is often at a premium, converting a garage can be a practical way to gain extra living space without extending the footprint of the building.

Internal Structural Alterations

Removing internal walls to create open-plan kitchen-dining-living spaces is a popular project across Wembley. Where load-bearing walls are involved, building regulation drawings must show the structural beam that replaces the removed wall, its bearing points, and the temporary support arrangements during construction. Our team regularly prepares these drawings for Wembley properties and works with trusted local structural engineers to ensure the designs are safe and efficient.

Wembley-Specific Considerations for Building Regulation Drawings

Several factors specific to the Wembley area influence the building regulation drawings we prepare for local projects. Our familiarity with these factors is one of the key advantages of choosing a local firm for your drawing requirements.

London Clay and Foundation Design

The predominant soil type across much of Wembley is London clay, which is classified as a high-shrinkage clay. This has significant implications for foundation design, particularly where trees are present near the proposed construction. London clay expands when wet and shrinks when dry, and the moisture content is affected by tree root systems that extract water from the soil. Our drawings specify foundation depths that account for the influence of nearby trees, following the NHBC guidelines for foundation design in shrinkable clay soils. In some cases, piled foundations may be necessary, and our drawings provide the details needed for these more complex foundation solutions.

The Wembley Growth Area

The Wembley Growth Area, centred on Wembley Stadium and Wembley Park, is subject to specific planning policies that can influence building projects in the vicinity. While these are primarily planning matters rather than building regulation issues, they can affect the design and therefore the building regulation drawings for projects within or adjacent to the growth area. For example, higher density development expectations may influence the scale of extensions or the design of replacement dwellings, which in turn affects the structural and thermal requirements documented in the building regulation drawings.

Party Wall Considerations

Many Wembley properties are semi-detached or terraced, meaning that extension and alteration projects frequently involve work adjacent to or affecting party walls shared with neighbours. While party wall matters are governed by the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 rather than the building regulations, the two processes interact because the party wall surveyor needs to review the building regulation drawings to assess the potential impact on the adjoining property. We prepare our drawings with party wall considerations in mind, clearly documenting the relationship between the proposed work and any shared walls or structures.

Frequently Asked Questions About Building Regulation Drawings in Wembley

How do I submit building regulation drawings to Brent Council?

Building regulation drawings can be submitted to Brent Council's building control department online through the Brent Council website, by post to the Planning and Building Control department at Brent Civic Centre on Engineers Way, or through a private approved inspector. ProStruct Design handles the entire submission process on behalf of our Wembley clients as part of our standard service, ensuring all required documents are correctly formatted and accompanied by the appropriate application forms and fees.

How much do building regulation drawings cost in Wembley?

Building regulation drawings in Wembley typically cost between £500 and £2,500 depending on the type and complexity of the project. A straightforward porch or garage conversion sits at the lower end, while a large double-storey extension or a complex loft conversion with structural alterations will be towards the higher end. We provide fixed-fee quotations before commencing any work so you know exactly what you will pay. Building control fees payable to Brent Council are separate and additional.

What types of projects need building regulation drawings in Wembley?

Common projects requiring building regulation drawings in Wembley include rear and side extensions, loft conversions, garage conversions, internal structural alterations such as removing load-bearing walls, new kitchens and bathrooms involving drainage changes, replacement windows that do not use a FENSA-registered installer, and any work involving electrical installations in kitchens or bathrooms. Essentially, any building work that affects the structure, fire safety, thermal performance, or services of a dwelling will require building regulations compliance.

Do I need planning permission as well as building regulations in Wembley?

Planning permission and building regulations are separate legal requirements in Wembley, as everywhere in England. Many home improvement projects fall under permitted development rights for planning purposes but still require building regulations approval. Conversely, some projects need planning permission but may be exempt from building regulations. In the Wembley area, the ongoing regeneration and specific planning policies in the Wembley Growth Area may affect permitted development rights for certain properties. We recommend checking both requirements at the start of your project.

How long does building control approval take in Brent?

For a full plans application, Brent Council building control has a statutory 5-week period to examine and determine the application, which can be extended to 8 weeks with your written agreement. In our experience, well-prepared applications are often determined within 3 to 4 weeks. If the building control officer raises queries, the clock may be paused until the queries are resolved. Using a private approved inspector can sometimes result in faster turnaround times. Building notice applications allow work to start 48 hours after submission but provide no pre-approval certainty.

Can ProStruct Design handle both planning and building regulations for my Wembley project?

Yes, we provide a comprehensive service covering both planning application drawings and building regulation drawings for projects in Wembley. Many clients benefit from this combined approach because it ensures consistency between the two sets of drawings and allows us to manage both submission processes simultaneously. This is particularly valuable for projects in the Wembley Growth Area where specific planning policies may influence the design, and for properties near conservation areas where design sensitivity is important.
